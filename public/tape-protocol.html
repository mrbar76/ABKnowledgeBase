Tape Protocol.
Forge

Seven circumference sites, one number each. Waist at the navel is the primary measure. The rules below matter more than the number itself — the value only means something when every week is taken the same way.

Take every measurement this way
Same time

Morning, fasted, after the bathroom, before training or water. Body size drifts all day.

Snug, not tight

Tape flat to skin, no gap, no dent. If it leaves a mark it's too tight.

Level

Tape parallel to the floor, all the way around (the neck is the one exception).

Relaxed

Don't flex, brace, or suck in. Measure at the end of a normal breath out.

Twice

Measure each site 2–3×; log the reading you can repeat. Round to 0.1 in.

Same side

Arm and thigh are one side only — pick your right side and keep it forever.

Where each site is

The seven sites, top to bottom
1

Neck

neck_in
Where
Base of the neck, just below the Adam's apple, where the neck meets the shoulders.
How
Tape sits a touch lower at the front than the back — the one site that isn't level. Loose, resting on the skin.
Tip
Keep your head straight ahead, shoulders down. Don't tuck the chin.
2

Shoulders

shoulders_in
Where
All the way around the widest point of the delts, at the top of the shoulder line.
How
Arms hang relaxed at your sides. Tape level, around the fullest part front and back.
Tip
Don't roll the shoulders forward or back — stand as you normally stand.
3

Chest

chest_in
Where
Around the fullest part of the chest, tape crossing at nipple line, under the armpits.
How
Arms down and relaxed. Measure at the end of a normal breath out — don't puff the chest up.
Tip
Level all the way around; check the tape isn't riding up your back.
4

Arm — relaxed

arm_relaxed_in
Where
The largest part of the upper arm (mid-bicep), roughly halfway between shoulder and elbow.
How
Arm hanging at your side, not flexed. Let it rest naturally; measure straight around.
Tip
Right arm every time. A relaxed arm reads smaller than a flexed one — keep it relaxed for a fair trend.
5

Waist — at navel

waist_inPrimary
Where
Straight around at the belly button — not the narrowest point, the navel line.
How
Stand relaxed, feet together. Don't suck in or push out. Measure at the end of a normal exhale.
Tip
This is the headline number Forge trends first. Consistency here matters most — same posture, same breath, every week.
6

Hip

hip_in
Where
Around the widest part of the hips and glutes, below the waist.
How
Feet together, weight even. Tape level all the way around the fullest point.
Tip
Look in a mirror side-on to find the true widest line before you read it.
7

Thigh

thigh_in
Where
The largest part of the upper thigh, just below the glute fold.
How
Stand tall, weight even on both legs. Tape level, straight around the thigh.
Tip
Right leg every time. Pick a fixed spot below the fold so you hit the same line weekly.
Log it in Forge

One entry, all seven

  1. On the Body screen tap + Log, then open the Tape Measurements section — a tape row needs no weight, just the sites you took.
  2. Enter each number in inches to one decimal: waist_in, chest_in, arm_relaxed_in, shoulders_in, thigh_in, hip_in, neck_in.
  3. Waist is the anchor. If you only have two minutes, take waist. Forge trends it first.
  4. Forge shows change vs your baseline and a 4-week trend per site, and pushes the numbers to Hevy (arm & thigh fill both sides there).
